Travel advisory and civil war disclaimer

Myanmar is not a normal travel destination right now. This page explains what the conflict means in practice, where to find authoritative advice, and the limits of what a small educational site like this one can tell you.

The short version

Check your own government's travel advice and your embassy's security updates before making any plan. Do not rely on this website for safety information, and do not travel to areas under active fighting or to regions that require permits you do not hold.

What is happening

Myanmar's civil war intensified after the military seized power in a coup on 1 February 2021, overthrowing the elected government and detaining its leaders. The conflict pits the military against pro-democracy resistance forces — including the People's Defence Forces — and a wide range of ethnic armed organisations that have fought for autonomy and political rights for decades.

Fighting has spread across much of the country, causing civilian deaths, the destruction of homes and infrastructure, and severe disruption to education, healthcare and the economy. As of mid-2026, UNICEF reported that nearly 3.8 million people were internally displaced and around 16.2 million needed humanitarian assistance. Those figures are updated regularly and should be treated as a snapshot, not a fixed number.

The human cost falls hardest on ordinary families, and particularly on children, who face interrupted schooling, food shortages and unsafe living conditions. A lasting peace will require protecting civilians, allowing humanitarian access, and a political settlement that addresses the aspirations of the country's many communities.

If you are planning a trip

  • Read your government's advisory in full, not just the headline level. Advisories name specific townships and roads that are off limits, and they change without notice.
  • Register with your embassy if your country offers it, and keep the contact details to hand.
  • Check your insurance. Most policies exclude areas under travel advisories, and medical evacuation from remote regions is difficult even in quiet periods.
  • Expect the plan to change. Flights, permits and road access can be suspended with no notice, and internet and mobile networks are sometimes cut in conflict areas.
  • Think about where your money goes. Some Burmese and international organisations argue that tourism revenue indirectly supports the military; others argue that visitor income sustains communities with few other options. It is a real debate, not a formality.
  • Do not cross into restricted areas, and do not photograph military positions, checkpoints or personnel.

What this site is and is not

This is a small educational project about Myanmar's culture, history and landmarks. The information is provided for general informational purposes only. While we try to keep it accurate, we make no representations or warranties of any kind, express or implied, about its completeness, accuracy, reliability or suitability for any purpose.

We are not a travel agency, we do not book anything, and we cannot verify conditions on the ground. Nothing here should be read as an endorsement of travel to Myanmar at any particular time, or as advice on whether it is safe to do so.

By using this site you acknowledge that you do so at your own discretion, that you will seek additional information from official and reliable sources, and that you will exercise caution when interpreting the content provided here.

Our position

We do not endorse or support any political group, faction or armed actor in the conflict. Our aim is to describe the country and its history fairly, to be explicit about what is contested, and to point readers towards sources that are in a position to give current, authoritative advice.
