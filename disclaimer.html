Myanmar's ongoing civil war intensified after the military seized power in a coup on February 1, 2021, overthrowing the elected government and detaining political leaders. The conflict involves the Myanmar military, pro-democracy resistance forces, including the People's Defense Forces, and various ethnic armed organizations that have fought for greater autonomy and political rights for decades. Fighting has spread across many parts of the country, causing civilian deaths, destruction of homes and infrastructure, and severe disruptions to education, healthcare, and the economy. According to UNICEF, nearly 3.8 million people were internally displaced by the end of June 2026, while 16.2 million people needed humanitarian assistance. The conflict has created immense hardship for ordinary families, particularly children, who face interrupted schooling, food shortages, and unsafe living conditions. A lasting peace will require protecting civilians, providing humanitarian assistance, and pursuing a political solution that addresses the country's long-standing conflicts and diverse communities' aspirations.
